### 1. **[Follow the Gradient: Tracing Safety Degradation Back to Training Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37481v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 83/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-27
- **作者**: Saket Reddy, Amro Abbas, Anshuman Suri et al.
- **标签**: `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `cs.CL`
- **AI摘要**: 提出SimpleLoRIF方法追溯并过滤导致LLM安全退化的训练数据
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Fine-tuning Large Language Models (LLMs) on instruction data can quietly erode their safety, even when no training example looks harmful. Because this safety-degrading data looks benign, standard content filters struggle to catch it. We tackle this problem by developing SimpleLoRIF, a scalable gradient embedding method that identifies fine-tuning examples whose gradients align with unsafe behavior. By simplifying a low-rank attribution method and restricting backpropagation to the final four transformer blocks, SimpleLoRIF can embed datasets on the order of a million examples, boosting gradient attribution from a small-scale analysis tool into a practical filter for full post-training datasets. We first validate it on emergent misalignment, where we find that gradients can connect insecure-code training data to the broadly harmful behaviors that appear after fine-tuning, with AUC as high as 0.865 in the gradient space of misaligned models. We then use SimpleLoRIF to build SafeTulu, a version of Tulu 3 with the 20% of examples most gradient-aligned with unsafe responses removed. Training on SafeTulu reduces GCG attack success rate by up to 22% across Qwen, Llama, and Olmo, outperforming both LLM-as-a-judge and prior gradient-based filtering, while largely preserving or even improving capability. Finally, we analyzed the removed data and identify possibly under-studied sources of safety degradation, such as tool-execution failures and narrative arithmetic. We release SafeTulu along with a Hugging Face Space that lets researchers build their own customized versions of SafeTulu to fine-tune on, such as by changing the removal fraction or filtering only certain subsets.
</details>

### 2. **[Watermark Basins: Black-Box Detection of Distilled LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37266v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 83/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-27
- **作者**: Hongyan Chang, Hady Elsahar, Tom Sander et al.
- **标签**: `cs.CR` `cs.CL` `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 提出水印盆地机制检测蒸馏大模型
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Proprietary language models and their reasoning traces are vulnerable to API-based distillation, where an adversary trains a student model on the teacher model's outputs. To obtain evidence of distillation, model providers can watermark the teacher's outputs, aiming to leave a radioactive signal in the student's outputs. Existing black-box detectors search for such radioactivity using token-level statistics of the suspect model's outputs. However, token-level signals are not always detectable, for instance when the distillation traces are sampled at a low temperature. In this work, we discover the watermark basin, a persistent signal that enables more reliable watermark detection: during fine-tuning, watermarked teacher traces embed a distinct, key-specific geometric signal in parameter space that lies orthogonal to the ordinary task-specific fine-tuning subspace. Building on this, we introduce Watermark Basin Alignment (WM-Basin), a black-box watermark detector that queries the suspect model through its API, without access to its weights or logits. WM-Basin maps generated text to gradient representations using a fixed clean probe model, projects out the ordinary fine-tuning task directions, and tests whether the residual gradient aligns with the watermark basin induced by the watermark key. We evaluate WM-Basin extensively across diverse teacher models and student architectures (from 135M to 8.2B parameters) distilled on mathematical reasoning. At a strict 0.1% false-positive rate, WM-Basin detects 79% of distilled models at n-gram=1 and 45% at n-gram=3, substantially improving over the state-of-the-art token-level baseline (29% and 1.3%, respectively). Because the watermark signal resides in a persistent basin, it survives post-distillation modifications including quantization, pruning, and reinforcement-learning post-training.
</details>
- **评分理由**: 创新性检测蒸馏大模型，实验严谨，实用性强。

### 3. **[Mechanistic Failure Modes of Weight Pruning in Transformer Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37126v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 83/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-27
- **作者**: Nabeelah Faumi, Deshan Sumanathilaka
- **标签**: `cs.LG` `cs.CL`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示模型剪枝的机理失效模式
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Pruned language models are judged almost entirely by accuracy and perplexity, which assumes that preserved output behaviour implies preserved internal computation. We test this assumption with a factorial pruning study on GPT-2, crossing two importance criteria (magnitude, random), two component types (attention heads, MLP neurons) and three sparsity levels (3%, 50%, 70%). Using the fully characterised indirect object identification (IOI) circuit as ground truth, we measure circuit integrity with activation patching, path patching and direct logit attribution. We find two qualitatively distinct failure modes. Magnitude head pruning &gt;= zeroes the circuit: output-facing heads are removed, and the model falls to near-chance. Magnitude neuron pruning at 30% inverts it: all 26 circuit heads remain intact, but Name Mover contributions flip sign, and the model systematically prefers the wrong name (17.7% accuracy). Perplexity ranks it as less damaged than random head pruning (74.6 vs 140.1). A replication of the Greater-Than circuit shows that the critical component type depends on the circuit's architecture. These results show that output metrics and structural checks can both miss mechanistic damage, motivating circuit-level causal analysis as a complementary evaluation of compressed language models.
</details>

### 4. **[On the Sparsity of Direct Preference Optimization: Weight Disentanglement in the NTK Regime](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37085v1)**

- **作者**: Kensuke Sasaki, Issei Sato
- **标签**: `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示DPO稀疏性机制并证明双重稀疏性
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has been widely adopted for aligning language models with human preferences. Recent empirical work has revealed that DPO induces remarkably sparse parameter updates, yet no theoretical explanation for this phenomenon has been offered. We analyze DPO's optimization dynamics under a neural tangent kernel linearization and show that the logistic structure of the DPO loss gives rise to an implicit maximum-margin bias. Building on this observation, we introduce the concept of dual sparsity: the DPO update direction is a sparse linear combination (data-level sparsity, driven by support vectors) of sparse gradient feature vectors (parameter-level sparsity, arising from gradient cancellation between preferred and dispreferred responses). <summary>原始摘要</summary>

### 4. **[Multi-Label Agreement: A Flexible Framework for Inter-Annotator Reliability](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37292v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 75/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-27
- **作者**: Tim Menzner, Jochen L. Leidner
- **标签**: `cs.CL`
- **AI摘要**: 提出多标签一致性评估的灵活框架
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Reliable evaluation of inter-annotator agreement (IAA) is important for assessing the consistency of annotation processes. While established IAA metrics such as Cohen's kappa, Fleiss' kappa, and Krippendorff's alpha are commonly applied in single-label settings, multi-label tasks require additional decisions about how full label sets, joint selections, and joint omissions should be compared. In this work, we present Multi-Label Agreement (MLA), a flexible framework for evaluating agreement in multi-label annotation tasks. MLA preserves the full binary label vectors assigned to each item and uses average pairwise Hamming agreement, thereby accounting for both joint selections and joint omissions. Its pooled-marginal form is not a mathematically independent coefficient: it is a chance-disagreement-weighted average of label-wise Fleiss/Scott-type coefficients and is closely related to Krippendorff's alpha with normalized Hamming distance. The framework supports pooled-marginal, Gwet AC1, and fixed-baseline normalizations, yields item-level, label-level, and corpus-level results, and includes a conditional minimum-agreement diagnostic. Through a worked example and Monte Carlo simulations, we show how the resulting values depend on label prevalence, the declared label inventory, and the selected model of expected agreement. We also provide a Python implementation. MLA thus offers a transparent and interpretable framework for multi-label agreement analysis without claiming that one normalization is universally preferable.
</details>
