<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Preference optimization ranks final answers and trajectory RL maximizes aggregate returns, yet neither credits the local change that turns a flawed reasoning state into a better one. We introduce Lifted State Policy Optimization (LSPO), which establishes this finite, cost-adjusted transition as the primary unit of learning. ### 2. **[Latent Compaction with Test-Time Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20582v1)**

- **标签**: `cs.CL` `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 提出潜空间压缩方法以优化KV缓存
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Long contexts enlarge key--value (KV) caches, increasing memory use and attention costs during generation. Compressing these caches must preserve information for unseen questions. We introduce **Latent Compaction**, which learns a compact document-specific cache through test-time training. We initialize cache entries using reconstruction importance, teacher attention, and key-space diversity. ### 3. **[When Does Momentum Help in Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20413v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 78/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-14
- **作者**: Kaifei Wang, Zikang Shan, Liwei Wang et al.
- **标签**: `cs.LG` `cs.AI` `math.OC`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示动量在RLVR中的加速机制
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Momentum is a classical tool for accelerating convex optimization and a standard component of modern large language model training. Yet recent empirical work has questioned the usefulness of first-moment momentum in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR). We study this question through a bandit formulation of RLVR with bias-corrected exponential moving average (EMA) momentum, where the EMA coefficient $β$ controls how quickly the weights on past gradients decay. When policy gradients are evaluated exactly, we prove that increasing $β$ within a range that prevents overshoot strictly accelerates optimization. When gradients are estimated from sampled responses, a positive expected gain can be obscured by sampling fluctuations. Bandit simulations quantitatively validate these predictions. RLVR experiments on Qwen3-4B-Base using SGD with EMA and AdamW support our principle of choosing larger coefficients in practical LLM post-training. On Qwen2.5-32B with AdamW, simply increasing $β_1$ improves mean validation accuracy by nearly $2$ percentage points over the conventional choice, demonstrating the principle's effectiveness at a larger model scale.

<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Existing approaches typically rely either on fixed data-agnostic transforms, such as Hadamard rotations, or on calibration-time optimization of learned rotations. <summary>原始摘要</summary>

Despite remarkable architectural diversity, these methods often share a common input representation: raw coordinate-value pairs $(x, u(x))$. We ask whether inputs aligned with PDE differential structure can improve learning. Across benchmark problems and architectures, $\partial$-NO consistently improves prediction accuracy, with learned orders that reflect a representation of known physics modulated by noise and finite-sample constraints.

<summary>原始摘要</summary>
