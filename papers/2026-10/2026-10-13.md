<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

### 2. **[Adaptive DHiSparse: Speculative Decoding over a Load-Adaptive KV Hierarchy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13708v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 82/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-09
- **作者**: Naji Alazhar, Wassel Alazhar

<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Long-context serving runs out of KV memory before the hardware reaches its useful throughput. HiSparse stores history in host memory and fetches only the selected entries, but it uses one cache budget at every load and does not handle draft-model speculative decoding such as DSpark and DFlash2. On DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 500K context and concurrency 512, Adaptive DHiSparse with DSpark delivers 1.85$\times$ the output of resident serving with DSpark. In a load step, optional residency reduces host fetches from 14.2% to 1.4% of selected rows at low load and releases memory under pressure.

### 3. **[Inject a Turn, Not a Token: Learning Where to Redirect LLM Reasoning for Self-Correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13672v1)**

- **作者**: Jiwon Jeon, Myungsik Cho, Jeonghye Kim et al.

<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Large language models struggle to reliably identify and correct errors in their own reasoning. Unlike prior training-based approaches that fine-tune an LLM to generate revisions or feedback, we learn where to intervene in failed rollouts and determine how to redirect them. To choose where to intervene, we train a 130K-parameter MLP head on a frozen encoder using only final-answer correctness. It outperforms self-correction and reflection-token injection baselines under matched correction budgets, transfers across reasoners and to GPQA-Diamond without retraining, and complements GRPO and self-correction methods.

### 4. **[A Critical Look at Power Sampling when Test Time Sharpening LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13653v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 82/100 | **日期**: 2026-10-09
- **作者**: Abiel Malkani Talwar, Christopher Nemeth, Henry Moss
- **标签**: `stat.ML` `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示幂采样本质是提取高概率序列而非逼近分布
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has substantially improved LLM reasoning, yet growing evidence suggests it mainly performs distribution sharpening: concentrating probability mass on reasoning paths the base model already produces. Recent work therefore seeks RLVR's gains without needing reward signals, by sampling from the power distribution $π_α\propto π_{\text{base}}^α$ via Markov chain Monte Carlo or sequential Monte Carlo. We show that these samplers fail to converge to $π_α$ under realistic compute budgets, and that their gains are largely recreated via simple Best-of-$N$ with appropriate grading . This suggests they succeed by selecting high-probability sequences, not by approximating $π_α$, raising a natural question: is the power distribution itself a worthwhile target? We test this hypothesis directly by fine-tuning the base model to match $π_α$, sidestepping sampler convergence issues entirely. Despite approximating the target far more faithfully than existing samplers, this strategy yields gains that are noisy and specific to certain models and sampling tasks. Together, our results suggest that the value of power sampling lies not in faithfully targeting $π_α$, but in the simpler goal of extracting sequences the base model already deems likely.
</details>

<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Discourse particles subtly shape the flow of discourse by reflecting conversational context and interlocutor attitudes, often leaving the truth-conditional meaning untouched.
