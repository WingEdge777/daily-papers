### 1. **[Spectral and Subspace Dynamics of LoRA in Supervised Fine-Tuning and Reinforcement Learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08414v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 83/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-05
- **作者**: Chenruo Liu, Zihan Wang, Yijun Dong et al.
- **标签**: `cs.LG` `stat.ML`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示LoRA在SFT与RL中的动力学差异并提出优化策略
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) is commonly used in both supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and reinforcement learning (RL), often under similar optimizer settings. Yet, how the different dynamics of the two stages manifest in the low-rank increment that LoRA optimizes remains unclear. Tracking the singular spectrum and subspaces of LoRA updates on mathematical reasoning tasks, we find that RL concentrates the spectrum at every learning rate, while SFT expands it at moderate learning rates; moreover, RL rotates the singular subspaces much faster than SFT at small learning rates. Interpolating between SFT and RL one ingredient at a time, we pinpoint group-mean baseline subtraction as the source of both contrasts. A quadratic multi-index model updated via LoRA unveils the underlying mechanism: group-mean baseline subtraction tilts the balance between two competing forces, coherent fitting and gradient noise, toward spectral concentration and faster subspace rotation, while forcing the risk-favorable learning rate to be low. Guided by these dynamics, we tailor LoRA updates to each stage: in SFT, we flatten the spectrum of the full update to spread adaptation across singular directions, whereas in RL, we flatten the part of the update outside the current LoRA increment span to promote subspace rotation. Experiments on mathematical reasoning tasks across multiple models show that the respective configurations for SFT and RL yield the best performance among the compared methods in most evaluated settings.
</details>

### 2. **[StrataFlux: Memory-Hierarchy-Native Low-Bit LLM Inference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08363v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 83/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-05
- **作者**: Jake Hyun, Ziyu Zhao, Jae-sun Seo et al.
- **标签**: `cs.LG` `cs.DC`
- **AI摘要**: 提出StrataFlux框架，突破LLM低比特量化精度瓶颈。
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Quantization reduces the memory and bandwidth demands of large language model (LLM) inference but can degrade accuracy; offloading higher-precision data can instead stall generation. We introduce StrataFlux, a framework that challenges this tradeoff by recovering accuracy lost to low-bit weight and key-value (KV) cache quantization while retaining quantization's memory and speed advantages. StrataFlux co-designs refinement representation, selection, and delivery to make host RAM and solid-state drives useful sources of additional precision. Its selective precision streaming reduces transfer volume, overlaps delivery with computation, and reuses fetched precision across decoding steps so generation need not wait for every transfer. At 128K context, Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct with 3-bit weights and 2-bit KV achieves comparable perplexity and speed to vLLM's 4-bit AWQ weights and 8-bit KV on RTX 5090, using approximately half the GPU memory. These results show that GPU-resident precision need not set the accuracy ceiling, nor offloading-tier latency dictate decoding speed.

<summary>原始摘要</summary>

We study token dynamics in deep Mamba models, with a particular focus on the attracting role of the first token. - **作者**: Guhan Chen, Bohan Li, Jun Rao et al.

- **AI摘要**: 提出CoRD方法解决策略蒸馏的状态盲区问题
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

On-policy distillation trains a student on its own rollouts, so states the teacher would reach but the student does not are seldom supervised. We introduce CoRD (coupled-rollout distillation), which keeps the on-policy loss and moves only the distribution of training states toward the teacher, drawing rollouts from a geometric interpolation of student and teacher through the maximal coupling of speculative decoding. ### 5. **[Muir: Linear Attention with Sensitivity-Allocated Gating and Matched-Filter Readout](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08376v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 82/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-05
- **作者**: Tushaar Gangavarapu, Gil Tabak, Clemens JS Schaefer
- **标签**: `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 提出Muir线性注意力模型
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Linear attention compresses context into a fixed-size key-value state for linear-time training and constant-memory inference. Gated delta-rule models decay this state and erase its prediction for an incoming key before writing a new value; Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) generalizes this with channel-wise decay and decoupled, full-rank erase and write gates. However, we show that state updates are far less sensitive to erasure than to writes: key activation and unit normalization concentrate key energy onto a sparse subset of channels, whereas value writes scale all channels, so the erase gate can be parameterized at lower rank. Furthermore, slow-decaying state rows accumulate much larger norms than fast rows, dominating unweighted readout and forcing stronger write-time erasure to suppress interference. Guided by this analysis, we introduce Muir, which fuses all gates into a sensitivity-allocated low-rank projection, re-weights state rows with a matched-filter readout gate, and reinvests the saved parameters in a wider feed-forward network. Because standard chunkwise algorithms become numerically unstable at the large chunk sizes needed to saturate wide matrix units, we design a parallel update for stable, efficient training. Across 166M-1.34B parameters, Muir lowers perplexity by 1.8-2.0% over GDN-2 and 2.5-3.6% over Transformer, raises commonsense accuracy by up to 1.9 points, and improves extractive recall over GDN-2. At 1.34B, Muir outperforms both baselines on multi-hop and multi-needle recall, extrapolates 2,560x from its 4K training length to 10M-token streams without perplexity drift, and maintains near-100% passkey retrieval to 1M tokens with static readout. Muir achieves these gains while matching GDN-2 throughput, reaching its 100B-token validation loss 1.2x faster, and training 2.7x faster than Transformer at 32K.
</details>
- **评分理由**: 创新性结合敏感度分配与匹配滤波，实验全面，提升显著

On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) improves reasoning by training a student on its own reasoning, using the same model conditioned on reference solutions as a teacher. Applying OPSD to multilingual reasoning requires the teacher to provide useful feedback across languages, even when the student makes mistakes in intermediate steps. We observe a phenomenon we call Teacher Drift: as these mistakes accumulate, the teacher may follow the student's incorrect steps instead of correcting them. <summary>原始摘要</summary>
