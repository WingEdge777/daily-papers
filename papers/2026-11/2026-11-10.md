### 1. **[Interpretability Is Not Model-Specific](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12675v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 84/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-09
- **作者**: Marcus Armstrong, Navid Ayoobi, Pradham Mummaleti et al.
- **标签**: `cs.AI`
- **AI摘要**: 证明大模型可解释性工具可跨模型迁移
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Interpretability tools are usually built for one model at a time: a refusal direction, a truthfulness probe, or a steering vector is found with labeled data inside the model it explains. We ask whether such tools can be carried to other models through a map between residual streams fit only on unlabeled prompts. We transfer a refusal direction, tested causally by addition and ablation, and two read-out tools, a correctness direction and a sentiment probe, across 110 pairs of models from five families and 1B to 72B parameters and between base and instruction-tuned models, with controls for specificity and five seeds. How much transfers depends on what the map was fit on. A map fit on ordinary questions recovers 54\% of the target's own refusal steering and 16\% to 77\% of its correctness gain; adding unlabeled prompts from the region where the tool operates raises these to 102\% and 88\% to 98\%, while control directions recover at most 1\%. We prove that a linear map behaves this way whenever two models correspond linearly on the data it covers, and a label-free coverage score derived from this result tracks recovery across more than 16,000 runs and explains earlier reports that maps between models fail across domains. Carried read-out tools outperform anything the target's unlabeled representations yield. Interpretability results, once found, can be reused across models at the cost of a few unlabeled prompts.

### 2. **[Is Persona Generalization Predictable?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12864v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 83/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-09
- **作者**: Olina Mukherjee, Arnav Goel, Aditi Raghunathan et al.
- **标签**: `cs.CL` `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示了大语言模型角色泛化中的普罗比线性规律
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Persona generalization occurs when fine-tuning a language model in one domain induces behavioral changes in others -- for example, the broadly unsafe behavior observed in emergent misalignment. We discover a surprising "on-the-line" phenomenon in persona generalization: in-domain and out-of-domain persona rates across various fine-tuning hyperparameters follow an approximately probit-linear relationship, and the specific probit-linear relationship is surprisingly shared even across model families and model sizes, including Qwen and Llama. Changing which layers are fine-tuned, however, can change the slope. For unsafe behavior, fine-tuning only later layers yields shallower slopes, retaining strong in-domain learning while reducing out-of-domain misalignment. This "on-the-line" phenomenon unifies unexpected, isolated observations of persona generalization within a quantitative framework for predicting and controlling behavioral transfer.

### 3. **[Fairness in Explainable AI Narratives: Decomposing Social Disparities in LLM-Generated Explanations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12592v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 83/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-09
- **作者**: Wannes Van den Bulck, Sofie Goethals, David Martens
- **标签**: `cs.CL`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示大模型生成可解释性叙事的社会偏见与公平性问题
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly used to translate technical explainable AI (XAI) artefacts such as SHAP into explanatory narratives (XAINs) for lay users. In high-risk domains, these explanations are often the main resource through which individuals can understand and contest adverse decisions. Yet LLMs are known to reproduce social bias, even from proxy variables alone, and fairness in XAINs remains unstudied. Because XAINs are bound to a ground-truth artefact, fairness concerns not only what a narrative says and how, but also whether it is equally faithful across social groups. We introduce fairness in XAINs as a new field of research and propose a framework that generates narratives under three conditions (protected attribute disclosed, withheld, or counterfactually swapped), allowing five comparisons that pinpoint the cause of disparities. A more faithful model removes the faithfulness disparities, and withholding protected attributes mostly removes the others, but neither suffices on its own. Fairness in XAINs should therefore be audited separately from the predictive model and its XAI artefact.
