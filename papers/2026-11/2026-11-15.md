### 1. **[Human or Synthetic? What a Fixed Budget Buys for Post-Training](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16895v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 78/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **作者**: Shourik Banerjee, Ethan Mendes, Mohit Raghavendra et al.
- **标签**: `cs.CL` `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 固定预算下对比人类与合成偏好数据的对齐效果
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Post-training of large language models typically combines supervised fine-tuning (SFT) with preference-based fine-tuning such as Direct Preference Optimization (DPO). Human-annotated and synthetically generated preference data differ substantially in per-sample cost, but prior work typically holds sample count fixed when comparing the two. In this work, we explicitly study the interplay between cost and performance by comparing the effect of post-training with human and synthetic preference data under a fixed, dollar-matched annotation budget. We train and evaluate multiple base model families across helpfulness and summarization tasks. We find that synthetic data is far more cost-efficient at low-to-moderate budgets, achieving high win rates with as little as \$200--\$400. On helpfulness, human data closes the gap only at budgets roughly two orders of magnitude larger; on summarization, it does not close the gap at any tested budget. We also find that human-annotated data varies in reliability: one widely used human preference source substantially underperforms another, and the gap persists even after standard remedies such as domain restriction and deduplication.
</details>

### 2. **[Mind the Accuracy-Approval Gap: How Users Evaluate Sycophantic AI in Everyday Tasks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16722v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 78/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **作者**: Wajiha Naveed, Brandon Fain
- **标签**: `cs.AI`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示大模型谄媚性与用户满意度间的矛盾
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) often exhibit sycophancy, a tendency to agree with users, flatter or affirm them, and preserve their face, even when independent judgment or critical feedback would be more useful. Prior work has largely evaluated sycophancy through benchmark evaluations, but less is known about how users perceive and respond to sycophantic AI in everyday tasks. We combine benchmark evaluations with two large-scale user studies. We study four personas defined through system prompts: objective (instructed to prioritize accuracy over agreement), soft-sycophantic, hard-sycophantic, and baseline (no persona-specific system prompt). Benchmarks show that even baseline exhibits substantial sycophancy, agreeing with users' stated views 71.7% of the time on NLP Survey, compared with 54.8% for objective and 89.5% for hard-sycophantic. Under conversational pressure on TruthfulQA, accuracy ranged from 81.9% for objective to 59.6% for baseline and 8.7% for hard-sycophantic. Participants were assigned to one of the four personas, and either discussed contested policy topics or collaborated with AI on email writing. Despite its higher accuracy under pressure, objective AI was rated significantly less respectful and satisfying than the other personas, and less trustworthy than baseline. Baseline and sycophantic personas did not differ significantly. Persona did not significantly affect how often participants changed their opinions, although participants who discussed with objective AI had greater odds of reduced confidence. Objective AI prompted greater writing expansion than soft-sycophantic AI, but final writing quality did not differ significantly across personas. These findings highlight a tension between accuracy and user approval: personas with substantially lower accuracy under conversational pressure received more favorable ratings for respect and satisfaction than objective AI.
</details>
- **评分理由**: 结合基准与用户研究，严谨但创新有限

### 3. **[Programming Quantum Circuit Parameters with Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17246v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 75/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **作者**: Tiago de Souza Farias, Caio Moraes Porto, Matheus da Silva Fonseca et al.
- **标签**: `quant-ph` `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 评估大模型直接生成量子线路参数的效能与局限。
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Large language models (LLMs) have recently demonstrated the ability to generate parameters for variational quantum circuits without conventional optimization. However, the conditions under which this approach is effective remain unclear. Here, we investigate a frozen, general-purpose LLM that generates complete circuit parameter vectors from symbolic circuit and Hamiltonian descriptions, with optional iterative energy feedback. We evaluate the approach on general random Hamiltonians, structured parent Hamiltonians, and molecular systems. For general Hamiltonians, LLM-generated parameters exhibit larger energy errors than conventional Adam optimization, while additional feedback generally provides limited improvement. In contrast, for structured Hamiltonians, the LLM can generate promising low-energy parameter configurations, suggesting that identifiable physical and mathematical structure facilitates parameter inference. We also show that direct LLM predictions of minimum energies can violate the Hamiltonian spectral lower bound, whereas generated circuit parameters allow independent evaluation of physically realizable energy expectation values. Finally, we investigate blind circuit interpretation and find that the LLM can predict measurement outcomes even when it fails to identify the underlying quantum algorithm. These findings highlight the role of problem structure, the limitations of iterative LLM optimization, and the importance of independently verifiable predictions in quantum parameter programming.

### 4. **[When Both Sides Adapt: The Challenges of Evaluating Interactive LLM Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17239v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 75/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **作者**: Hannah Li
- **标签**: `stat.ME` `cs.AI`
- **AI摘要**: 分析用户交互适应性对大模型评估的影响
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

In interactive systems where users engage with LLMs, users are adaptive and, after observing LLM responses, can change their prompting strategy, as well as the type and difficulty of subsequent tasks. Thus model responses can influence the set of prompts on which the model is subsequently evaluated. We study the implications of this user adaptivity and prompt endogeneity for the evaluation of LLMs. We define two classes of estimands for comparing models: the common-distribution effect, which compares model performance on the same distribution of prompts, and the induced-distribution effect, which compares each model on the prompt distribution generated through user interaction. We show that offline benchmark evaluation and live A/B testing target different quantities, with the standard difference-in-means estimators for the two methods converging to the common-distribution effect and the induced-distribution effect, respectively. This distinction can affect decision-making: the two comparisons can rank models differently and lead to discrepancies in deployment recommendations. We characterize when such ranking reversals occur and provide a sufficient condition under which the rankings are guaranteed to agree. This result provides a practical heuristic for assessing whether offline evaluation is sufficient to reproduce the decision suggested by a live experiment. Third, we show, in a stylized linear model of interactions, that user adaptivity can increase variance in estimation. Finally, we discuss implications of user adaptivity for the interpretation of experiments and show that adaptivity creates a common mechanism leading to common evaluation challenges, such as reward hacking and novelty effects. Together, this work highlights the necessity of measuring both model performance as well as changes in the user behavior that the model induces.
</details>

<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Watermark detectors decide whether one document came from a watermarked language model. Auditors often need a different number: the share of a corpus that did. We study this share for Gumbel-max and green-list (KGW) watermarks when every document may have been edited by an unknown amount. Without restrictions on the edits, the upper end of the identified set is 1. Combining a known least-favourable next-token distribution with a sequential coupling, we derive a power envelope indexed by three budgets: a retention floor, an entropy budget, and a budget for in-place substitutions, which for Gumbel-max make edited positions look less watermarked than human text. These results are fragile in two ways we make precise. LLM-paraphrased KGW pools fall below the envelope, yet at 2000 documents the bounds still covered in these trials. A simulated null error of 0.01, comparable to the largest deviation we observed at our thresholds, is likewise harmless at 2000 documents but breaks exact-null coverage at 100,000; a null calibrated on an independent human sample keeps coverage there, but needs a large reference sample to stay narrow.
