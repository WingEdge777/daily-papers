### 1. **[On the Difficulty of Sequence-Level Supervised Learning for Autoregressive Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16736v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 78/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **作者**: Mathieu Blondel, Michaël E. Sander, Germain Vivier-Ardisson et al.
- **标签**: `cs.LG` `stat.ML`
- **AI摘要**: 揭示自回归模型序列级损失函数的理论缺陷
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

During the supervised learning phase, autoregressive language models are typically trained by minimizing the negative log-likelihood of the training data, which decomposes as a sum of token-level cross-entropies via teacher forcing. This objective is simple and efficient but potentially suffers from so-called exposure bias: a mismatch between the way the model is trained and the way it is used at inference time. Can we instead design sequence-level loss functions that incorporate inference-time decoding procedures, such as greedy decoding or autoregressive sampling? We show that two natural attempts lead to loss functions that are fundamentally flawed: unbounded below. We further show that these two flawed loss functions are intimately connected: using autoregressive Gumbel tricks, the sampling based loss is the expected greedy loss under Gumbel perturbations. Since the pathology stems from the dependence of the decoded sequence on the model parameters, we analyze the update direction obtained by applying a stop gradient on the sampling procedure, leading to a moment matching direction. While it is not the gradient of any loss function, we prove that it defines a valid descent direction in function space for the autoregressive negative log-likelihood. Despite these theoretical insights, the moment matching direction suffers from high variance. We conclude that teacher forcing may well be ``all you need'' for supervised learning of autoregressive models.
</details>
- **评分理由**: 理论分析深刻，但缺乏实验验证

### 2. **[When Expert Disagreement Hurts: Prestige and Selectivity in LLM Decision Revision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16694v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 78/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **作者**: Yupeng Tang, Mingfeng Lin
- **标签**: `cs.AI` `cs.CL`
- **AI摘要**: 评估大模型在专家异议下的决策修正能力
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

When language models revise a decision after another agent disagrees, neither reversal counts nor final accuracy shows whether they are fixing mistakes or abandoning correct answers. We introduce a controlled two-pass audit that holds the question, the evidence, and the model's initial answer fixed and varies only the revision context: neutral reflection, anonymous disagreement, or disagreement attributed to a domain expert. For binary decisions the accounting is exact: revision raises accuracy only if it is selective enough, reversing wrong answers more than $a/(1-a)$ times as often as correct ones, where $a$ is initial accuracy (nine times at 90%). Across five models and 1,989 evidence-grounded decisions in medicine, science, and law, no model clears this bar under expert disagreement, and accuracy falls by up to 16.1 points. The expert label, a prestige cue, adds reversals for four of the five models, yet its effect on accuracy ranges from a 5.2-point loss to a 2.4-point gain, so reversal counts are a poor proxy for harm. With identical arguments from both sources, the label still raises reversal for three proprietary models and in a pooled analysis of four newer models. Models also respond to stated source reliability. Revision pipelines should therefore be evaluated with paired, conditional metrics, per model and protocol.
</details>

### 3. **[Sketching Language Models for Context Attribution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17313v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 75/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **作者**: Christopher Liaw, Aranyak Mehta, Grigoris Velegkas et al.
- **标签**: `cs.DS` `cs.AI` `cs.LG`
- **AI摘要**: 提出高效的大语言模型上下文归因草图算法
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Context attribution asks how removing parts of a prompt changes a language model's output. We study leave-one-out (LOO) attribution for a prompt of $n$ tokens and a fixed measurement function. Computing all LOO values directly requires $n$ model evaluations. Under a stability assumption, our algorithm estimates all LOO values to error proportional to $\varepsilon$ with constant probability using $O(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ evaluation calls. To complement our upper bound, we prove a matching $Ω(\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon)}/\varepsilon)$ lower bound on the number of evaluation calls in an oracle model. Empirically, we verify coefficient decay consistent with stability and evaluate the performance of our sketching algorithm on a small language-model retrieval task. Technically, our result is based on a sketching algorithm for arithmetic circuits whose Taylor coefficients satisfy the stability condition recently introduced by Gunn [Gun26]. A direct adaptation of their forward differentiation sketches would require $\widetilde{O}(\log(n)/\varepsilon^2)$ evaluation calls. Our key observation is that we can apply forward differentiation to the backpropagation circuit, with only constant overhead over a forward sketch of the same Taylor degree. This reduces the variance of the sketches and is key to our improved evaluation bound. Our lower bound builds on the techniques of Amsel, Chen, Duman Keles, Halikias, Musco, and Musco [ACDH+26, SIAM Journal on Matrix Analysis and Applications] for lower bounding the number of matrix-vector queries to estimate the diagonal elements of random matrices.
</details>

<summary>原始摘要</summary>

We construct a coding setting with multiple sub-tasks that allows us to evaluate goal-seeking behavior and the selection of sub-goals. <details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Pre-trained encoders fine-tuned on natural language inference (NLI) and other language understanding tasks keep much of their accuracy after the input is perturbed, e.g. by removing all nouns or shuffling the words. ### 2. **[Optimal Single-History Tokenization Disambiguation for Provably Secure Linguistic Steganography](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17091v1)**

- **作者**: Feng Jiang

<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Provably secure linguistic steganography assumes that the receiver recovers the sender's tokens, but a text channel delivers only text, and ambiguous tokenizations break extraction. Existing fixes change the distribution, synchronize whole pools of tokens at a capacity that collapses with large vocabularies, or track several token histories. Coin projection keeps 91-93% of the string entropy for the coder at every vocabulary size; with the full vocabulary, it turns 2.16-6.85 times as much entropy into message bits as SyncPool and recovers every completed message.
