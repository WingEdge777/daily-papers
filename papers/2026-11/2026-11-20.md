### 2. **[Sightline: Calibrated Data-Flow Summaries for LLM-Integrated Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26221v1)**
- **评分**: ⭐ 84/100 | **日期**: 2026-11-19
- **作者**: Zihao Xu, Yuekang Li, Ruitao Feng et al.
- **标签**: `cs.PL` `cs.AI`
- **AI摘要**: 提出Sightline用于大模型应用的数据流摘要
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Programs increasingly call large language models (LLMs), filling prompt placeholders with program values and consuming the output. Such a call crosses the boundary between natural language and programming language (NL/PL). To follow data across it, an analysis needs a data-flow summary of which placeholders the output uses, but no code describes what the model does. Analyses of these LLM-integrated applications therefore taint every output with every input, creating spurious flows, or ask a second model to label each call, which is costly. We prove, and mechanize in Lean 4, that a placeholder flows to the output of a transformer, to first order, only through the attention that reads it (no reading, no flow), and that a faint reading or sensitivity cannot certify that a placeholder does not flow. Unlike reading, the log-probability gradients and Fisher signals of existing methods vanish on confident outputs, and single-span removal fails on duplicated content. Our tool, Sightline, reads a recorded call in one forward pass, learns from labels which reads are uses, and calibrates the pruning to miss at most a chosen fraction $α$ of true flows, on average over call sites, when deployment is exchangeable with calibration. On 2,103 unseen repository families and four models, Sightline is the most accurate summary against the seven closest methods and a hidden-state probe. Calibrated at $α$ = 1%, it misses about 1% of true flows and prunes 43.9-65.6% of spurious flows, 1.6 to 2.6 times as many as the best baseline, and still 43.8-60.1% when calibrated on another collection. Through an open surrogate, it reads models treated as closed about as accurately as they read themselves, without their labels. In the information-flow control of an LLM agent, it removes about half of the labels from inputs that do not flow.
</details>
- **评分理由**: 理论与实验扎实，解决LLM数据流分析难题

- **AI摘要**: 揭示自一致性认证中的相关性偏差并提出修正方法
<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

Self-consistency certification treats repeated sampling from a language model as sequential evidence accumulation and stops when an anytime-valid e-process crosses a threshold, guaranteeing finite-sample Type I error control by Ville's inequality. Prior constructions assume sampled rollouts are conditionally independent. We show this assumption fails in practice: reasoning streams are positively correlated through shared reasoning prefixes or across-prompt heterogeneity, changing not merely certificate efficiency but the hypothesis certified. Modeling dependence via a per-unit latent state (the random-effects / intraclass-correlation structure) induces exchangeable, equi-correlated increments with correlation $ρ$. Correlation splits the single null into a realized null about the drawn belief and a population null about average model behavior, which coincide only when $ρ=0$. We show: (i) the standard betting e-process remains anytime-valid for the realized null, not the population null; (ii) read as a population-null test, a suitable mixture has asymptotic crossing probability at least $π=P(μ(Z)&gt;0)$, potentially exceeding nominal $ε$; (iii) the within-unit mean's effective sample size saturates at $1/ρ$, so extra within-unit rollouts cannot restore population validity; and (iv) a prompt-level e-process restores population validity, with optimal allocation at one rollout per prompt under a pure rollout budget. This correctness result identifies which reliability claim an anytime-valid certificate actually supports under dependence.
